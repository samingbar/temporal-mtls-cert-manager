Operational Concern Specification: Credential Supervisor
Overview
Concern Name: Credential Status: Implementing Owner: TODO(author): assign Created: 2026-09-03 Updated: 2026-09-09

Executive Summary
Customers often miss the courtesy emails sent by Temporal to rotate credentials and often do not have their own automation to respond. This results in application downtime, buggy manual processes, and support churn.

A Credential can be encapsulated in such a way to orchestrate automate credential rollover required to connect to Temporal Cloud Namespaces without any downtime. It makes a slice of credential material durable.

It will use Temporal itself to:

Safely establish trust with Temporal namespace.
Guarantee the rollout of that trust to associated Temporal SDK Clients.
Retire old credentials once they are no longer needed.
It will be flexible enough to support:

Multiple credential types, though Certificate Authority (CA) TLS Certificates are prioritized for now
Temporal Namespace collections
different PKI toolchains and integrations
It will not:

Coordinate with other Credentials supervising the same Temporal Namespaces
Implement guarantees inside the target processes for connectivity
Act as a operational probe/monitor for connectivity to Temporal Namespaces
Goals & Success Criteria
Primary Goals
Goal 1: Manage Certificate Authority (CA) trust for a scoped set of Temporal Namespaces
Goal 2: Integrate with other PKI infrastructure
Goal 3: Trigger and monitor rollout of trust to Temporal SDK Clients without downtime
Goal 4: Safely retire old credentials once they are no longer needed
Acceptance Criteria
 A timed rollout of a new CA completely replaces the old CA
 A manual rollout of a new CA replaces the old CA
 Workloads are not impacted by the rollout
 The Temporal Namespaces under management can be modified safely
Current State (As-Is)
What happens today without this?
Courtesy emails are sent to remind customers to rotate credentials, but these are ignored unseen.

Pain points
Certificates are not rotated in a timely manner so connections fail. Downtime results.
Manual processes with Temporal UI and PKI infrastructure are required to rotate credentials, leading to missed steps or bugs.
Desired State (To-Be)
Approach Overview
A Credential acts as an interactive, behavioral entity for an otherwise static bit of credential material such as a TLS certificate or API Key. A Credential will be initially scoped and activated once by the maintainers of the Temporal Namespaces it interacts with. It represents exactly one credential; e.g. a TLS certificate that acts as a CA for an arbitrary number of Temporal Namespaces. A Temporal SDK Worker will need to always run for the Credential to perform its duties and maintain its integrity. A Credential needs appropriate permissions to access any resources it mutates outside of its own compute boundaries (e.g. the Kubernetes Namespace where it is running). The current state and progress of each Credential operation should be visible to eligible maintainers. The Credential's scope may be safely increased or decreased at any time.

A Credential will schedule or force rotations within its current scope, performing these steps:

Coordinate and read new credential material (e.g. a new public key) from arbitrary PKI infrastructure
Establish trust with the scoped Temporal Namespaces while retaining the old trust
Cascade the new trust to related Temporal SDK Clients
Remove old trust from related Temporal Namespaces
Retire the old trust once it is no longer needed
Schedule the next rollover to repeat this process
Key Capabilities
Capability 1: Temporal Namespaces placed within scope are arbitrary. They can be all, many, or one.
Capability 2: A Credential can be interacted with directly, run on a schedule or on-demand, and abstracts away compute and PKI implementation details.
Capability 3: A Credential can represent different credential types, including Certificate Authority (CA) and TLS certificates or API Keys.
Capability 4: A Credential can support compensations, such as stop or rollback, in the event of a failure.
Technical Approach
Design Decisions
Decision	Rationale	Alternative Considered
A Credential is top-level and user facing.	A single point of entry is needed to interact and view credential concerns within a scoped collection of Namespaces.	Singular credential supervision across all Temporal Namespaces
A Credential represents exactly one concrete credential.	The credential it manages is itself the root of other credentials.	Multiple credentials can be managed by a single CredentialSupervisor but leads to complex failure conditions.
A Credential only impacts the deployments in the Kubernetes cluster in which is running.	Cross-cluster support leads to confusion about who is the authority for the credential.	A centralized platform cluster could speak into services that need the credential
A Credential orchestrates the high-level process but does not have an opinion about PKI toolchain or deployment implementations.	CredentialSupervisor needs to be flexible with integration in customer environment.	Kubernetes-specific or Hashicorp specific concerns leaking into steps.
A Credential is not a monitoring or health check tool, but can inform such a probe.	It encapsulates the steps needed to orchestrate a safe rollover but makes no guarantees about connections impacted by it.	CredentialSupervisor could maintain probes and try to correct faults with a rollover.
The Credential Workflow has no Workflow Execution or Run Timeout.	It is a long-lived entity. Business waits and child rotation work use explicit timers, and Continue-As-New bounds Event History.	An infrastructure timeout would turn entity lifetime into an accidental failure policy.
Credential cancellation requests cancellation of an active Rotate child and waits for its bounded revert path.	Rotate owns compensation for work it has performed. The parent must not abandon that work on shutdown.	A detached child can outlive the entity that owns the operation and make final state ambiguous.
This implementation phase stops at Workflow and Activity contracts plus the Credential Workflow.	The durable boundary can be tested with fake adapters before external credentials and cluster access are introduced.	Implementing external adapters in the same phase increases review scope and requires live environment details.

TemporalSDK: Java
Behaviors:
State: Capture Activity responses, child Workflow responses, Update outcomes, and attributable failures in one initialized state object. Temporal Event History is authoritative for execution state. Temporal Cloud and Kubernetes remain authoritative for external inventory.
Steps:
Local Activity: mergeOptions is the first Workflow action in every run. It overlays request values on environment configuration, validates the merged value, and returns the effective options used by Workflow code.
Activities in parallel: fetch the scoped Temporal Cloud namespace certificate inventory and the matching local Kubernetes Deployment inventory. With Kubernetes integration disabled for local development, the future adapter returns an empty, explicitly skipped inventory.
Compute the next rotation from the earliest managed namespace certificate expiration minus rotationOffset. Clamp a past recommendation to immediate. If no managed namespace has a certificate, remain ACTIVE with no scheduled rotation until scope or options change.
Wait for the next scheduled instant or an accepted Update. Refresh externally owned inventory immediately before a rotation decision.
Start a Rotate child with a stable child Workflow ID derived from Credential identity and the required operation ID. The child receives an immutable StartRotateRequest snapshot. Scope or option changes made while it runs apply to the next rotation.
If rotationTimeout elapses, request child cancellation. Rotate owns its internal revert. Wait no longer than another rotationTimeout for cancellation and revert to finish, then record the bounded outcome.
On native Credential cancellation, use the same child cancellation and bounded-wait path before allowing cancellation to close the parent.
After a successful child result, append the complete summary to state, drain in-flight handlers, and Continue-As-New with current options, deduplication IDs, and the complete summary history. deactivate completes instead of continuing as new.

Configuration / Deployment
Kubernetes API Access From The Workflow
Decision: The Credential and Rotate Workflows do not call the Kubernetes API directly. They schedule Activities on the CredentialOperations Worker, and those Activities use the official Kubernetes Java client to perform all Kubernetes reads and mutations. This keeps network I/O, ServiceAccount token access, wall-clock time, and API retries outside deterministic Workflow code.

Client configuration: The Java client uses in-cluster configuration. It discovers the local API server and authenticates with the projected token and cluster CA for the Pod's temporal-operations-sa ServiceAccount. The Worker does not mount a kubeconfig or hold credentials for another cluster. Typed clients are used for core Secret and apps/v1 Deployment resources; the custom-objects client is used for secrets.hashicorp.com/v1beta1 VaultPKISecret resources.

Kustomize boundary: Kustomize is deploy-time tooling only. It renders and applies the Worker Deployment, ServiceAccount, ClusterRole, and ClusterRoleBinding. Neither Workflow nor Activity code shells out to kubectl or kustomize, and those binaries are not runtime dependencies of the Worker image.

Activity operations: The Vault-backed implementation performs the following calls through the Java client:

| Operation | Kubernetes access | Purpose | |-----------|-------------------|---------| | Discover managed certificate resources | Cluster-wide list of VaultPKISecret resources using credentials.temporal.io/ca-team=<team> as the label selector | Finds the live workload inventory for this team and reads each resource's destination Secret and spec.rolloutRestartTargets | | Resolve configured Deployment selectors | Cluster-wide list of Deployment resources, only when KubernetesOptions.DeploymentSelectors is configured | Supports the generic Credential inventory described above; the Vault path derives target names from rolloutRestartTargets instead | | Capture and verify rollout state | get each named target Deployment | Captures the pre-cascade generation and verifies the later rollout reached the new generation with all desired replicas available | | Force immediate VSO reissue | patch each selected VaultPKISecret, then delete its destination Secret | Records the rotation attempt and causes Vault Secrets Operator (VSO) to recreate the missing Secret; a missing Secret is treated as an idempotent success | | Verify VSO and certificate state | get the VaultPKISecret and destination Secret | Checks the VSO Ready condition and confirms that tls.crt was issued by the expected new CA |

The Credential Worker does not patch application Deployment resources. VSO performs that patch from the VaultPKISecret's rolloutRestartTargets after it writes changed Secret data. The Credential Worker only reads Deployment state to verify the resulting rollout.

Failure and retry behavior: Kubernetes calls are bounded Activities with explicit timeouts. Transient API server, throttling, and transport failures are retryable. Invalid selectors, missing rollout targets, malformed VaultPKISecret resources, and authorization failures are configuration or policy failures and must be recorded in Workflow state rather than retried indefinitely. Mutating Activities must be safe under Temporal's at-least-once Activity execution: patches carry a stable rotation identifier, and deleting an already-absent destination Secret succeeds.

The baseline cluster-wide grant is:

apiVersion: v1
kind: ServiceAccount
metadata:
  name: temporal-operations-sa
  namespace: temporal-operations
---
apiVersion: rbac.authorization.k8s.io/v1
kind: ClusterRole
metadata:
  name: credential-operations-kubernetes
rules:
  - apiGroups: ["secrets.hashicorp.com"]
    resources: ["vaultpkisecrets"]
    verbs: ["get", "list", "patch"]
  - apiGroups: ["apps"]
    resources: ["deployments"]
    verbs: ["get", "list"]
  - apiGroups: [""]
    resources: ["secrets"]
    verbs: ["get", "delete"]
---
apiVersion: rbac.authorization.k8s.io/v1
kind: ClusterRoleBinding
metadata:
  name: credential-operations-kubernetes
subjects:
  - kind: ServiceAccount
    name: temporal-operations-sa
    namespace: temporal-operations
roleRef:
  apiGroup: rbac.authorization.k8s.io
  kind: ClusterRole
  name: credential-operations-kubernetes
This is cluster-scoped because the Worker discovers and verifies workloads in namespaces it does not know before discovery. The label selector narrows client behavior, but Kubernetes Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) does not enforce that label as an authorization boundary. This role can therefore read or mutate the listed resource types outside the owning team's scope. In particular, get on Secret returns the complete object, including tls.key, even though the Activity only inspects tls.crt. Treat temporal-operations as a high-trust namespace and restrict who can deploy or exec into these Pods. A customer that cannot accept this cluster-wide grant must use namespace-scoped Role and RoleBinding resources plus an explicit workload inventory; that is a different discovery trade-off from the cluster-wide label-based default.
